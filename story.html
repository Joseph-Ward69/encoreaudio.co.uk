Our story

Why Encore Audio exists, from the person who started it.

Records in the front room

I grew up in the early 70s with music all around me. My mum, my sisters and my brother were always buying records, and my mum and dad each had their own radiogram: Dad's Blaupunkt in the front room, which was phenomenal, and a smaller Blaupunkt in their bedroom. Radio, 7-inch singles, LPs: that vinyl sound is where I come from. I'm a vinyl head through and through.

Vinyl is expensive in 2026, so CDs became my fallback, and I have a big collection of both. I ripped my CDs to my NAS so I could play them anywhere in the house. That's when the trouble started.

Music software that got in the way

I tried the well-known music servers. I even bought the dedicated hardware. One gave me what I loved about CDs and records, the feeling of holding the album and reading the liner notes, but it came with constant updates. Too often, when I sat down to listen, I had to wait for the software or the box to update first. Another sounded lovely but took an age to scan my library.

The last time it happened, I found myself looking at yet another product to buy, and I thought: no, I'm not doing that.

Why not just stream?

Because streaming isn't owning. You pay to licence music that can disappear the day a label and a service fall out. An album can be available in the US but not in the UK. And the quality isn't always there. I believe we should own our music, and listen to it whenever we like, the way we want to hear it.

So I built Encore Audio

I wanted something that just works. It plays my own library, bit-perfect, to the streamers at home, without a parade of updates between me and the music. And it comes out of the door with me, on my phone, through a good DAC and a pair of wired headphones, without downloading everything before I leave. (Call it the purist in me, but I'm not big on Bluetooth headphones.)

Film and TV have always mattered to me too, so a sister project, Encore Cinema, grew out of Encore Audio.

What I care about
  • Bit-perfect sound. Your music as it was recorded, nothing lost on the way.
  • Your library stays yours. Private and secure by design, on your own computer.
  • It just works. Music first, never a wait for an update.

Who I am

I'm Winston Joseph-Ward. Music isn't just something I listen to: I used to sing professionally, and went on to write and produce my own music too. Along the way I worked with Meat Loaf, Fun Lovin' Criminals and the Osbournes, among others.

By day I'm an enterprise architect, and I've worked in IT for 30 years. I was taught to code, but I'm not a natural coder: give me a strategy and I'll tie it to technology. That's my world.

Encore Audio is built with AI. Some people have a problem with that. I don't. It's the way of the world now, and it's how one person with a clear idea can build something like this. I bring the vision, the architecture and the ears; AI writes code alongside me.

Where we're going

I have big ideas, including bringing back the liner notes and credits that made holding a record so special. More than anything, I want people to enjoy their music again: to hear the quality of their own collection through good IEMs or over-ear headphones and a decent DAC, which needn't cost the earth any more. And at home, to kick back, shoes off, lights down low, and listen the way they want to.

That's what Encore Audio is all about. If you hear it and you like it, I hope you'll enjoy it too.

Winston Joseph-Ward
Founder, Encore Audio Ltd
